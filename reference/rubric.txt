=====================================================================
KARGO HIRING RUBRIC v1 - Product Manager (PM) & Senior PM (Sr PM)
=====================================================================
Built from: 2 JDs + 8 past-hire CVs + rating table
(5 Exceeds, 2 Meets, 1 Below). Purpose: shortlist people who look
like Arjun's best hires, not people who merely match the JD.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THREE PATTERNS IN HIGH-RATED HIRES THAT THE JD DOES NOT SCREEN FOR
---------------------------------------------------------------------

PATTERN A - DOMAIN-FIRST, FUNCTION-SECOND
They did the operational work themselves BEFORE moving into their
current function. All 5 "Exceeds" hires have this; none of the
Meets/Below hires do.
  - Rohan: 3 yrs in family CHA firm (JNPT docs, customs), then engineer
  - Sunita: 4 yrs freight documentation, then consulting
  - Aditya: 2 yrs port-terminal sales at JNPT, then SaaS sales
  - Meghna: 2.5 yrs freight-forwarder docs/client desk, then SaaS CS
  - Lavanya: 3 yrs carrier ops at Mahindra Logistics, then PM
  - Not present: Vikram (HR tech), Rahul (fintech marketing),
    Preetham (e-commerce backend; touched 3PL APIs, not the operation)
JD gap: Sr PM JD calls logistics familiarity an "advantage"; PM JD only
asks for "curiosity". Neither asks for having done the job.

PATTERN B - BUILDS THE FIX UNASKED, AND IT GETS ADOPTED BY OTHERS
Saw a broken process, built the fix without being told, and the CV
shows others adopting it (users, teams, "now standard").
  - Rohan: BoL verification prototype built over a weekend, 30 users
    in a month; Excel shipment tracker adopted by 12-person team
  - Sunita: redesigned intake workflow over a weekend after vendor
    format change; SOPs adopted in full within 60 days
  - Meghna: 30-60-90 onboarding framework now used by full CS team
  - Lavanya: Excel->Tableau visibility dashboard adopted by 2 teams
  - Aditya: first case-study programme, now team's main collateral
JD gap: PM JD says "you'll build those" (future tense). It never asks
for proof the person has already done this unprompted.

PATTERN C - CLOSES THE LOOP ON FAILURE AND WRITES IT DOWN
When something breaks, is lost, or is killed, they document why and it
changes how the team works.
  - Aditya: post-mortem on lost freight account, now standard practice
    before account qualification
  - Lavanya: killed 2 features on usage data and documented why;
    wrote outage post-mortem (internal + customer versions)
  - Meghna: wrote bug report for undocumented limitation, ran 6-week
    resolution with the customer informed throughout
  - Sunita / Rohan: SOPs, code review, on-call rotation introduced
JD gap: PM JD says "shipped things, killed things" but nobody is asked
for the written rationale or the change in team practice.

ANTI-SIGNAL (do NOT reward): credentials and scale. Vikram (XLRI MBA,
CPO cert, Reforge) and Preetham (3,200-person employer, multiple
certs) were rated lower than hires with modest credentials.

  - Location is a gate: in-office Mumbai or willing to relocate.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
5. LIMITS - READ BEFORE TRUSTING THIS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
- The clean separation above is circular: the rubric was derived from
  these same 8 people. Expect a smaller gap on real applicants.
- n = 8, only 2 were PMs, only 1 was rated Below. Treat the patterns as
  hypotheses. Re-check thresholds after scoring the real 60 CVs.
- Only CVs and the rating table were provided. The hire profiles
  (what stood out, interview notes, outcome lines) would sharpen this.
- Do not score on name, gender, age, college brand, or city of origin.
